This is the working document behind the Version 2.0 rewrite: thesis, arc, chapter briefs, style sheet, author-confirmed facts, verified sources, and legal language rules. Chapter drafts were written against this document, then revised in a full consecutive pass. It is kept in the repository so a human editor can see the reasoning behind the structure.

## 1. What the book is

**Title:** BURN IN (Version 2.0)
**Author:** Patrick Tran
**Audience:** ambitious high performers: founders, entrepreneurs, physicians, executives, creators, professionals. People whose competence conceals distress. The physician perspective stays because it is Patrick's life, but every lesson must generalize without being told to generalize.

**Old thesis (2021):** Burnout comes from the outside. Burn in by changing your mindset, building financial freedom, and needing the outside world less.

**Version 2.0 thesis:** Outside and inside both matter. Agency does not mean everything that happens to you is your fault; it means that whatever has happened, your next move still matters. The goal is not to escape your life. The goal is to build a life you can stay in. Burn In means building an inner operating system, relationships, financial margin, boundaries, habits, and external systems that can hold ambition without letting it consume the person generating it.

**Arc:** ACHIEVEMENT → CRACKS → COLLAPSE → SURVIVAL → UNDERSTANDING → REBUILDING → INTEGRATED AMBITION.

**Governing images (use each with discipline):**
- Fire and its container (fireplace vs. living-room floor). Intro states it; Chapter 14 develops it. Others may touch it once.
- The bush with a hollow where a body went through it, and flowers growing from the hollow. Chapter 5 shows it briefly; the Epilogue returns to it fully. Nowhere else.
- The hand of God: once, in Chapter 5, as Patrick's experience ("it felt as though"). Nowhere else.
- The candle that becomes a fire (original figure): Introduction only.

The finished book must read as one continuous book by one author, not a set of essays. It must feel like the same author several years later, after reality stress-tested his philosophy.

## 3. Legal language rules

**The fraud matter.** Approved framing, in Patrick's voice:
- "Several of the commercial real-estate transactions I had entered were connected to a person I had trusted. That person was later convicted of bank fraud in federal court and sentenced to prison."[endnote to public record]
- "I believed I could be facing nearly ten million dollars in personal exposure."
- "I imagined bankruptcy."

- Never: "he defrauded me," "he stole from me," "he set out to ruin me," "we were all victims," "every deal was a fraud," "I was tricked into lying," "the bank came after me for ten million dollars," "I owed ten million dollars."
- Do not describe the other person's character, appearance, personality, or private life. Not a villain. Not a friend either. A person at the center of transactions.
- Do not reproduce the 2021 blurbs or acknowledgments that praise him.

**Former spouse.** Not present. Not alluded to.

**Medical claims.** Use only Section 4 wording. Otezla: "The prescribing information states that treatment with Otezla is associated with an increased incidence of depression" and describes the monitoring advice. Never "Otezla caused." Ayahuasca: case reports of mania after ayahuasca in people with bipolar vulnerability exist; a systematic review found such episodes rare; people with personal or family history of psychosis or mania are advised to avoid it. Never "ayahuasca caused."

**Third parties.** Paul DeWolf and his family: public record plus the 2021 edition's quotation from his father, which was given for that edition; flag re-permission. Do not name the convicted men in Paul's case in body text. Nurses, staff, mentors: first names or roles only, as in 2021, and nothing unflattering. The former real-estate community, coaching group, and podcast are not named.

**Suicide narrative safety.** State that he jumped from the roof of an eight-story parking garage, expected to die, and landed in a bush rather than on concrete. Nothing about how he got to the roof, what time, which side, how high in feet, how he fell, what broke. Do not romanticize. Do not present the attempt as sacred or heroic. Recovery is slow and unglamorous.

## 5. Style sheet

**Register.** A professionally edited trade book. Intelligent, candid, specific, self-aware, funny when appropriate, medically literate, entrepreneurial, reflective, willing to admit error. Not sterile, not corporate, not an Instagram carousel, not a sermon, not a trauma memoir.

**Paragraphs.** Real paragraphs of three to seven sentences carry the book. One-sentence paragraphs are for earned emphasis only: no more than roughly one in eight paragraphs. Never a run of one-line paragraphs. Never every sentence on its own line. Fragments are not rhythm.

**Sentences.** Vary length and shape. Long sentences that turn a corner; medium sentences that carry information; short sentences only where the weight has been earned. No three consecutive sentences with the same first word. No lists disguised as prose ("Self-knowledge. Health. Relationships. Humility.") except where a list is honestly a list, and then rarely.

**Banned or rationed constructions** (the validator counts these):
- "It did not… It did not… It did not…" sequences; "It did not/didn't" as sentence opener ≤3 per chapter.
- "You can…" as sentence opener ≤6 per chapter; "I was…" ≤10; "Sometimes…" ≤4.
- "The goal is not X. The goal is Y." ≤2 per chapter. "The lesson is…" ≤1. "The truth is…" ≤1. "This matters because…" ≤1.
- "Not X. Not Y. Not Z." fragments ≤4 per chapter.
- "Performing fine" ≤3 per chapter, and it is Chapter 5's phrase first. Develop the idea in narrative; do not let it become a slogan.
- "Hand of God": once in the whole book.
- Em-dashes ≤10 per chapter. Prefer commas, periods, colons, parentheses.
- Questions ≤10 per chapter. Italics ≤25 spans per chapter. List items ≤24 per chapter.
- Subheads (`##`) ≤4 per chapter; prefer scene breaks (`* * *`). No `###`.
- Vocabulary to avoid: delve, tapestry, testament to, it's important to note, game-changer, unpack, navigate (as metaphor), embrace, empower, thrive, authentic, vibrant, profound, ultimately, "here's the thing," "let that sink in." Ration "journey," "resilience."
- No motivational slogans, no fake profundity, no rhetorical-question cascades, no chapter-ending "moral" boxes as a formula, no epigraphs.

**Voice test.** Before finishing a chapter: could these paragraphs have appeared in a thousand generic self-help books? If yes, rewrite with Patrick's actual scenes, observations, contradictions, jokes, and professional experience. Do not fabricate scenes or dialogue. Where a scene is needed and the source has none, write reflection, not invented memory.

**Humor.** Patrick is irreverent. Keep it: Costco, the velvet pillow, "hot shit," patients saying he looks twelve, the Tesla joke. Humor is not allowed to deflect in Chapter 5 or to belittle mental illness anywhere.

**Numbers.** Spell out numbers in prose where a trade book would (fourteen centimeters, eight-story, ten million dollars). Statistics may use numerals with an endnote.

**Tense and time.** Past tense for narrative; present tense for reflection. Avoid calendar dates in the crisis period. Use "the first edition," "when I wrote the first version of this book," "in the years after."

**Addressing the reader.** Second person is fine, in moderation. Patrick does not lecture; he reports what happened and what he thinks now.

**Endnotes.** Marker `[^cNN-k]` immediately after the punctuation of the sentence that needs it (e.g., `[^c06-2]`). Definitions live in `manuscript/20-notes.md`, grouped by chapter. Endnotes carry sources; they do not carry jokes or asides. No inline URLs in prose.

**Figures.** Markdown image on its own line, path relative to `manuscript/`, alt text is the caption: `![Caption sentence.](../assets/figures/fig-04-ruler-1cm-to-14cm.png)`. Place a figure where the prose has just earned it. Each figure appears once in the book. Do not describe the figure in the prose ("as the illustration shows").

**Headings.** File begins with a single H1: the chapter title only (no "Chapter 5"). Numbering and part titles come from `book.yml`.
