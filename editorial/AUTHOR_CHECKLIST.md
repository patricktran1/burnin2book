# Author Checklist — what only you can decide

Everything in this repository that could be built, checked, or verified from the supplied materials has been. This page is what is left, written as questions rather than as a report. `editorial/FINAL_QA.md` has the evidence behind each item; this is the working list.

Roughly: a few hours of your time, one session with a publishing attorney, half a day of source re-checking, and then a copyedit and a cover.

## A. Decisions only you can make (start here)

**A1. The endnote that names the convicted individual.**
Your name never appears next to his in the body text. The body says only: "Several of the commercial real-estate transactions I had entered were connected to a person I had trusted. That person was later convicted of bank fraud in federal court and sentenced to prison." The endnote at the back cites the federal press release, which identifies him by name.

- [ ] Keep the endnote as written (names the case and the defendant, as the public record does), **or**
- [ ] Reduce it to the release title and URL without the name, **or**
- [ ] Remove the endnote and make the body sentence unsourced.

There is a real trade-off. The endnote is what makes the claim verifiable rather than insinuating; dropping it makes the sentence softer but less defensible. Discuss with counsel before choosing.

**A2. Is there anything you are not free to say?**
This was written without knowledge of any settlement, litigation, lender agreement, or confidentiality obligation. Confirm that nothing in Chapters 5 and 10 breaches one. If something does, tell your editor which sentence — most of the chapter survives the removal of any single fact, because it deliberately narrates fear rather than events.

**A3. Chapter 15 is addressed to "Adrian, and to any child of mine who reads this later."**
The supplied materials do not say whether there are other children. Confirm the wording, or give the correct one.

**A4. Three disclosures, each of which you can still withdraw.**
The manuscript gives no dates for any of them, names no indication for the medication, and asserts no diagnosis.

- [ ] **Otezla.** Chapter 6 says you took samples of a drug you prescribe, around the period of the depression, and wonders whether it contributed. It states the label's depression warning accurately, including the fact that cuts against the hypothesis (two placebo subjects in the trials died by suicide and no one on the drug did). It never says the drug caused anything. Keep?
- [ ] **Ayahuasca.** Chapter 6 says: "In the years before the crisis, in ceremonial settings, I drank ayahuasca." No date, no place, no facilitator, no description of the experiences. Keep?
- [ ] **Bipolar vulnerability.** Chapter 6 says you "probably" have an underlying vulnerability, understood later and with help, and is careful to call it a vulnerability rather than a diagnosis. Keep, or adjust the hedging?

**A5. The suicide chapter.**
Read Chapter 5 straight through once, as a reader. It states that you went to the roof of an eight-story parking garage and jumped, expected to die, and landed in a bush rather than on the concrete, and it stops there — no date, no city, no route to the roof, no injuries, no hospital name. "Caught by the hand of God" appears once in the whole book, unexplained. Confirm that this is the level of detail you want in print, in both directions: nothing missing that you wanted said, nothing present that you did not.

**A6. Front matter.**
Supply the copyright year (currently 2026), ISBNs if any, and the publisher line. Confirm the dedication ("For Adrian. And for everyone who is very good at seeming fine.") and the reader's note.

## G. Things you may want to reconsider as the author

Not problems — editorial choices made on your behalf that you are entitled to overrule.

- **The first edition is quoted often and critically.** Nearly every chapter quotes something you wrote in 2021 and then takes it apart. It is the spine of the book's argument, and it is also, cumulatively, a lot of self-correction. If it reads as too hard on your younger self, the dial is easy to turn down.
- **"Victim mindset" is retired by name** in Chapter 8, with three reasons given. That is a public reversal of one of the first edition's central chapters.
- **The retraction of "the best thing that ever happened to me"** in Chapter 4, about Adrian's cancer.
- **The retraction of your definition of Hell** in Chapter 15 ("the achievement trap in a robe").
- **Chapter 15 ends on an ordinary morning**, not on a lesson. Several chapters end quietly on purpose. If you want a stronger close anywhere, say which.
- **Money is discussed without a single figure**, including your own losses and portfolio. Chapter 10 analyses the failure in detail — speed, leverage, liquidity, complexity, rooms, confidence — without one dollar amount except the feared "nearly ten million dollars in personal exposure," which is framed throughout as a belief rather than a debt.
