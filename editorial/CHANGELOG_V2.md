# Changelog — BURN IN, Version 2.0

Changes from the 2021 first edition (*Burn-In: A Doctor's Guide to Finding Happiness, Avoiding Burnout and Catching FIRE*) to Version 2.0 (*BURN IN: Building a Life You Can Stay In*).

## Thesis and audience
- Thesis revised from "burnout comes from outside; burn in by changing your mindset and building financial freedom" to "outside and inside both matter; agency means your next move still matters; build a life you can stay in."
- Audience widened from physicians to ambitious high performers generally; the physician perspective is retained as the author's life, not the book's address.
- Title simplified to *BURN IN*; the 2021 subtitle (doctors, FIRE) retired.

## Structure
- 11 chapters + foreword/afterword → Author's Note, Introduction, 15 chapters in four parts, Epilogue, Acknowledgments, About the Author, Notes.
- New chapters: The Achievement Bargain (3), The Cost of Performing Fine (5), More Than Burnout (6), Shame Loves Secrecy (7), Money Creates Margin, Not Meaning (10), Design Beats Willpower (12), The Burn In Protocol (13), Ambition After Collapse (14), What I Want My Children to Know (15), Epilogue: Still Here.
- Replaced: "The Victim Mindset" → "Agency Without Self-Blame" (8); "Egos and Superpowers" → "Ego, Confidence, and Blind Spots" (4); "The Meaning of Wealth" absorbed into 10 and 13; "Mindset Over Matter" absorbed into 6, 8, 9.
- Removed as chapters: "Making Money Work for You," "Playing to Win," the Foreword's financial framing, the Afterword's reading list.
- Retained with revision: "Who Am I, and How Did I Get Here?" (1), "Where Am I Going?" (2), "Here and Now" (9), "We're All in This Together" (11).

## Content added
- The financial and legal crisis, written with the three registers separated (what the author feared; what the public record established about another person; what remains unknown).
- The author's depression, hidden while functioning; nighttime terror; early-morning legal calls.
- The suicide attempt from an eight-story parking garage, survival in a bush, treatment and slow recovery, stated plainly without method or location detail.
- The bush revisited years later: the hollow and the flowers, the edition's governing metaphor.
- Retrospective examination of the 2021 passage minimizing the earlier parking-garage thought.
- Precise treatment of burnout (WHO/ICD-11) versus depression, anxiety, suicidality, bipolar vulnerability, sleep deprivation, medication effects (Otezla/apremilast, per prescribing information), and substance effects (ayahuasca, per case reports and a systematic review), with uncertainty stated as uncertainty.
- A money chapter built on margin, optionality, leverage, liquidity, complexity, due diligence, independent advice, and the seduction of rapid success.
- A practical synthesis (Connect, Recover, Build) and an "if you are where I was" section with the 988 Lifeline.
- A chapter addressed to the author's children.
- Endnotes with sources for every medical, scientific, legal, and historical claim; a fact-check log with labels; a legal-review log.

## Content removed
- All former-spouse references, anecdotes, and acknowledgments (see `CUT_MATERIAL.md` §1).
- All real-estate teaching, FIRE positioning, portfolio and transaction-volume claims, Wealthbound marketing, and community calls to action (§2).
- Praise pages and acknowledgments naming a business partner later convicted of bank fraud (§3).
- COVID-era framing (§4).
- Unsupported metaphysical certainty about souls, death, the universe's intentions, manifestation, and energy (§5).
- Redundant or immature-in-hindsight passages, including "Adrian's cancer was the best thing that ever happened to me" (retracted in text) and the "definition of Hell" (retracted in text) (§6).

- Michelangelo's David: the 2021 text said he "took over the project in 1521"; V2 gives the documented history (Agostino di Duccio 1464, Antonio Rossellino 1476, Michelangelo 1501–1504).
- Physician suicide: the unsourced "300 to 400 a year, double the general population" claim is replaced by the 2024 BMJ meta-analysis.
- Vietnamese proverb: corrected rendering ("Không ai giàu ba họ, không ai khó ba đời").
- Ram Dass quotation: attributed to the 2017 documentary *Ram Dass, Going Home* rather than to *Still Here*.
- Paul DeWolf: "Xbox" changed to "a game console" (news reports say a PlayStation 3); perpetrators unnamed.
