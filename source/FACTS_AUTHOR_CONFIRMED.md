# Burn In 2.0 — Author-confirmed facts and editorial constraints

This file records facts and decisions directly supplied by Patrick Tran for the Version 2.0 rewrite. Where the 2021 manuscript conflicts with these instructions, these instructions control the Version 2.0 treatment.

## Audience

The Version 2.0 audience is broader than physicians. It is for ambitious, high-performing people, including founders, entrepreneurs, physicians, executives, creators, and other professionals. Patrick's physician background remains part of the narrative, but the book should generalize beyond medicine.

## Former spouse

Patrick wants his former spouse removed from the new book. Do not name her, characterize her, attack her, diagnose her, or preserve relationship anecdotes merely because they were in the 2021 edition.

For Adrian's initial cancer story, rewrite from Patrick's perspective. Patrick is comfortable with wording such as noticing that one side of Adrian's abdomen looked different or recognizing the bump/asymmetry and acting on it. Do not manufacture an elaborate alternate history.

Delete passages that fundamentally belong to the former spouse, including the hospital-cafeteria anecdote.

## Real estate and financial-success positioning

Patrick does not want Version 2.0 to boast about making large amounts of money in real estate or to market him as a FIRE/real-estate guru.

Remove or substantially rewrite claims centered on a $22 million portfolio, rapid commercial real-estate growth, large transaction volume, Wealthbound, passive-income guru positioning, and FIRE as the central answer to burnout.

Money still belongs in the book, but the mature thesis is that money creates margin and optionality, not identity or meaning.

## Fraud / financial crisis

Patrick became involved in commercial real-estate transactions associated with a person who was later convicted and sentenced to federal prison for bank fraud.

During the period when the situation was unraveling, Patrick feared he could be facing nearly $10 million in personal exposure and possible bankruptcy. This is a description of what he feared at the time, not a claim that he actually owed $10 million.

The emotional importance of this episode is that Patrick believed he had failed catastrophically and that the financial/legal uncertainty destabilized his identity as a competent high performer.

Any public legal facts about the convicted individual must be sourced from authoritative public records and carefully separated from Patrick's subjective experience.

## Depression and suicide attempt

Patrick became severely depressed during the financial/legal crisis. He kept much of the depression to himself and felt that almost nobody fully understood how bad it was.

He experienced intense nighttime fear and would sometimes awaken with terror already present. Because attorneys he needed to speak with were in the Midwest, he often had to wake early for legal calls before work because of the time difference. He continued working and functioning professionally despite his internal state.

Patrick later attempted suicide by jumping from the roof of an eight-story parking garage. He expected to die.

He survived after landing in a bush rather than directly on surrounding concrete. The manuscript should state that he jumped and survived, but should not include an exact address, access instructions, calculations, graphic injury details, or method-optimizing information.

Years later Patrick saw the bush again. There was still a hollow/hole where his body had broken through it, and flowers were growing from that area.

Patrick experienced his survival as if he had been "caught by the hand of God." Use this spiritual interpretation sparingly, ideally once. Do not turn the chapter into a religious sermon and do not add unnecessary lines explaining God's intentions.

The flowers and the persistent hole are an important Version 2.0 metaphor: recovery does not erase what happened; life can grow around the wound.

Patrick is safe now. Do not repeatedly interrupt the manuscript or editorial workflow with safety checks directed at the author.

## Earlier parking-garage thought

The 2021 edition contains an earlier passage from surgical training in which Patrick thought about jumping from a parking garage and then minimized the thought by saying it probably was not "true" suicidal ideation.

Version 2.0 should not preserve that minimization uncritically. The later attempt makes the earlier passage an important example of how a high-performing, medically knowledgeable person can rationalize or dismiss warning signs in himself.

## Otezla / apremilast

Patrick had taken Otezla/apremilast samples around the period of his depression and wonders whether it might have contributed.

Do not state that Otezla caused the suicide attempt. Present it as one possible factor worth considering retrospectively and support any drug-label statement with authoritative prescribing information.

## Bipolar vulnerability and ayahuasca

Patrick later came to understand that he probably had underlying bipolar vulnerability. He also wonders whether ayahuasca use years earlier might have unmasked, triggered, accelerated, or interacted with that vulnerability.

Do not state that ayahuasca caused bipolar disorder. Present this as retrospective uncertainty and distinguish personal interpretation from established evidence.

## Spirituality

Retain meditation, humility, surrender, awareness, and subjective experiences of meaning where they serve the book.

Reduce unsupported metaphysical certainty from the 2021 edition. Prefer language such as "it felt as though" or "I experienced it as" rather than asserting unverifiable supernatural mechanisms as fact.

## Visual identity

Preserve the original stacked BURN-IN visual identity and the original illustration language. The extracted figures are supplied in `source_material/figures/`.

Do not redesign the book into generic startup/SaaS branding. Classify every original figure as KEEP, MODIFY CONTEXT, or CUT, and reuse original artwork whenever the revised concept still fits.

## Prose standard

The book must read like a professionally edited trade book, not AI-generated motivational fragments.

Use grammatical paragraphs, varied sentence and paragraph length, natural transitions, scenes, argument, specificity, humor when appropriate, and occasional short lines only when earned.

Avoid repetitive sentence openings and excessive constructions such as:

- "It did not..." repeated in sequence
- "You can..." repeated in sequence
- "I was..." repeated in sequence
- "Not X. Not Y. Not Z."
- "The goal is not X. The goal is Y." used as a constant tic
- constant one-sentence paragraphs
- generic LinkedIn/self-help cadence
